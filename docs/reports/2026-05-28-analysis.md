## How to read this report

A reader who is **not** an ML practitioner should be able to follow
this end-to-end. Every number below comes from one CSV:

- **[`docs/reports/joined-wide-table.csv`](joined-wide-table.csv)** —
  one row per M1G test image (550 total), columns =
  `Image_RID`, `True_Class`, and for each of the three models
  (`W76`, `XCE`, `YHP`):
    - `<exec>_pred` — the model's top-1 predicted class (one of 10)
    - `<exec>_conf` — how confident the model was in that prediction (0–1)
    - `<exec>_prob_<class>` — the model's probability for each of the
      ten classes (these sum to 1.0 per image)

Two derived summary tables are saved next to it:

- **[`joined-wide-table-summary.csv`](joined-wide-table-summary.csv)**
  — per-model headline numbers (samples, top-1 accuracy, mean
  confidence).
- **[`per-class-recall.csv`](per-class-recall.csv)** — for each model
  and class, how many of the 55 test images of that class the model
  got right.
- **[`per-class-confusion-long.csv`](per-class-confusion-long.csv)** —
  the full 10×10 confusion table per model in long form, ready for
  pivoting in any tool.

If a number in this report ever feels suspicious, open the wide table
and recompute it. Two `pandas` lines reproduce anything below; the
catalog also has the same files attached to analysis execution
[1012](https://localhost/id/27/1012@355-RZWY-9B9R) so they remain
fetchable after the worktree goes away.

## What we're comparing

The Modeler trained three models on the same 550-image training
partition ([M16](https://localhost/id/27/M16@355-RZWY-9B9R)) and
evaluated each on the same 550-image test partition (M1G). The
training and test partitions are from *different* Toronto source
batches, so a test image is never one the model was trained on —
unlike the `cifar10_labeled_split` / `cifar10_small_labeled_split`
datasets the Curator flagged in [tk-002](../../tacit-knowledge.md#tk-002),
which had 30%+ overlap.

| Run | Execution | Architecture | Epochs | Top-1 accuracy (n=550) | Mean confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **quick** | [W76](https://localhost/id/27/W76@355-RZWY-9B9R) | 32→64 channels, 128 hidden | 3 | **24.0 %** (132/550) | 0.27 |
| **default** | [XCE](https://localhost/id/27/XCE@355-RZWY-9B9R) | 32→64 channels, 128 hidden | 10 | **37.8 %** (208/550) | 0.51 |
| **large** | [YHP](https://localhost/id/27/YHP@355-RZWY-9B9R) | 64→128 channels, 256 hidden | 20 | **41.1 %** (226/550) | 0.81 |

A random guesser on this 10-class problem would get **10 %**; all
three models are above that. "Mean confidence" is the average of the
top-1 probability the model emitted — high mean confidence means the
model is willing to commit, not that it is right.

## Ranking the runs

**By raw accuracy: YHP > XCE > W76.** That ordering matches the
training story: more epochs and more parameters produced more
correct answers. But the gap from XCE to YHP is small (3 percentage
points, ~18 extra correct out of 550) compared to the gap from W76
to XCE (14 percentage points, ~76 extra correct).

**By confidence calibration: XCE > YHP > W76.** This is the more
interesting ranking, and the one a domain user would want to know
about before using these models for anything real. Calibration is
"when the model says it's 90 % sure, is it right 90 % of the time?"
You can read it directly off the wide table:

| Model | Mean conf when **right** | Mean conf when **wrong** | "Confidently wrong" (conf ≥ 0.8 but wrong) |
|---|---|---|---|
| W76 | 0.33 | 0.25 | **1** of 418 errors |
| XCE | 0.58 | 0.47 | **23** of 342 errors |
| YHP | 0.85 | 0.77 | **171** of 324 errors |

YHP is **the most accurate and the most overconfident**. When it's
wrong (which is 59 % of the time), it's wrong with the same swagger
it has when it's right (0.77 vs 0.85). For 171 of 550 test images
(31 %), YHP says it's at least 80 % sure of the answer and is wrong.
The Modeler flagged this in [tk-005](../../tacit-knowledge.md#tk-005)
and [tk-006](../../tacit-knowledge.md#tk-006): YHP memorised the
training set (train_acc hit 100 % by epoch 17) and the catalog's
prediction CSV is from the *final* memorised epoch (20), not from
the best validation epoch (13, where test_acc was 42.36 %). For a
practitioner using YHP for triage, that's the more important
property than the 3-point accuracy bump over XCE.

XCE is the **honest middle**. It's about right when it's confident
(only 23 of its 342 errors are confidently-wrong) and admits
uncertainty when it doesn't know (only 5 images get a confidence
below 0.2). W76 is honest too, but in the way an untrained model is
honest: it doesn't have an opinion (165 of 550 predictions are at
confidence < 0.2 — basically random).

## Where each model is right, wrong, and uncertain

The clearest single picture is per-class **recall** — for each true
class, what fraction of the 55 test images of that class the model
got right.

| Class      | W76    | XCE    | YHP    |
|------------|-------:|-------:|-------:|
| airplane   | 0.527  | 0.491  | 0.364  |
| automobile | 0.000  | 0.509  | 0.655  |
| bird       | 0.000  | 0.164  | 0.145  |
| cat        | 0.018  | 0.345  | 0.273  |
| deer       | 0.018  | 0.109  | 0.364  |
| dog        | 0.109  | 0.255  | 0.273  |
| frog       | 0.655  | 0.545  | 0.545  |
| horse      | 0.200  | 0.218  | 0.382  |
| ship       | 0.073  | 0.455  | 0.509  |
| truck      | 0.800  | 0.691  | 0.600  |

Three patterns jump out:

1. **W76's "everything is a frog or a truck."** The quick model
   collapsed onto two attractor classes. Frog gets 0.66 recall and
   truck gets 0.80 — but the wide table shows automobile→truck = 34
   of 55 cars (61.8 %) and ship→truck = 34 of 55 ships (61.8 %).
   W76 is essentially predicting "truck" for anything with hard
   edges and "frog" for anything blobby. This is what a model with
   too little training capacity looks like.
2. **XCE generalises broadly but flatly.** Every class except bird
   gets at least 10 % recall, but only frog and truck clear 50 %.
   No catastrophic failures.
3. **YHP is class-selective.** Vehicles (automobile, ship, truck)
   and large mammals (horse, deer) get a real lift; bird stays
   stuck at 0.145; cat actually *drops* relative to XCE (0.345 →
   0.273). The capacity that bought YHP the accuracy bump went
   into the easier classes; the hard classes didn't benefit.

## Does this match domain intuition?

A domain reader's first three intuition checks for CIFAR-10 are
usually **cat ↔ dog**, **automobile ↔ truck**, and **airplane ↔
ship**. All three show up in the top confusions of both XCE and YHP,
which is the right shape: these are the visually-overlapping pairs a
human would also confuse if shown 32×32 pixel thumbnails.

What surprises:

- **YHP's biggest pairwise confusion is bird ↔ deer (21 mix-ups).**
  That is *not* an intuitive pair — they're not visually similar to
  a human. Inspection of the per-class probabilities in the wide
  table shows YHP has learned a "small-with-background-detail"
  feature that fires on both: bird→deer happens 14 times (25 % of
  birds), deer→bird only 7 times. The model has not learned to
  separate the silhouette structure that would distinguish them.
- **Automobile ↔ truck is symmetric in YHP (9/9) but asymmetric in
  XCE (16/7).** XCE has a "if it has wheels, call it a truck" prior;
  YHP has actually learned to separate the two (both directions
  about equally likely when wrong).
- **Cat is the hardest class consistently** — 0.273 recall for YHP
  despite all its extra training. The wide table shows cat→frog,
  cat→dog, cat→bird, cat→deer all contribute roughly equally; the
  model never finds a stable signature for cat. This matches the
  well-known finding that cat is the hardest CIFAR-10 class even on
  much larger networks; here at 41 % overall accuracy, it's the
  bottleneck.

## Caveats a domain user should know

1. **Small test set.** 55 images per class is enough to see large
   effects (a 5-point recall gap is real) but not enough to trust
   small ones. The standard error on a 50 %-recall estimate at
   n=55 is about ±6.7 percentage points; differences of less than
   ~13 points between two models on one class are inside that
   noise band.
2. **The CSV is the final-epoch model, not the best-epoch model.**
   Per Modeler [tk-006](../../tacit-knowledge.md#tk-006), YHP's best
   test accuracy (42.36 %) was at epoch 13; the committed CSV is from
   epoch 20 at 41.09 %. The 3-point accuracy gap from XCE to YHP
   becomes 4.5 points if the comparison is "XCE final vs YHP best,"
   but that's not what's in the catalog and not what this analysis
   ranks. A best-epoch checkpointing change would be a fix-pass
   item, not an Analyst arc one.
3. **The Curator's leakage finding does NOT apply here.** The
   `cifar10_labeled_split` and `cifar10_small_labeled_split` datasets
   leak 30 %+ of test images back into train (Curator
   [tk-002](../../tacit-knowledge.md#tk-002)); the Modeler explicitly
   chose the Toronto-pair (M16 train, M1G test) to avoid that. Every
   accuracy and recall number in this report is on the leakage-free
   pair.
4. **Ground truth was filtered to execution `HSR`** per Curator
   [tk-001](../../tacit-knowledge.md#tk-001). The shipped
   `roc_analysis.ipynb` template uses a "Confidence IS NULL" filter
   that on this catalog silently picks the partial 500-row GT
   execution `854` instead, which would have evaluated each model on
   only 250 of the 550 M1G images and reported ~3 % higher
   accuracies. See [`findings/analyst/02-roc-notebook-picks-wrong-gt-execution.md`](../../findings/analyst/02-roc-notebook-picks-wrong-gt-execution.md).
   The pictures in the catalog (ROC curves, confusion matrices on
   the n=250 subset) from notebook execution
   [ZW0](https://localhost/id/27/ZW0@355-RZWY-9B9R) are still
   directionally correct — the relative ranking of W76 < XCE < YHP
   is the same — but the absolute numbers are from a smaller
   sample and slightly different from the n=550 numbers above.

## What this team got right, and what it didn't

**Got right:**

- The Curator's dataset audit caught the leakage trap before the
  Modeler picked a dataset, so the Modeler picked the clean Toronto
  pair without rediscovering the problem.
- The Modeler's three-run spread (3 → 10 → 20 epochs, small → large
  architecture) was the right shape for a "tell me what the pipeline
  is doing" question: the runs visibly differentiate, and the
  underfit → reasonable → overfit progression is exactly what a
  practitioner would want to see.
- The catalog has all three prediction CSVs (W96, XEE, YKP) as
  Execution_Assets with provenance links to their producing
  executions and weights, so this analysis is replayable.

**Missed or workaround-needed:**

- The roc_analysis notebook's GT-execution heuristic. The Curator
  predicted this in tk-001 and the analyst had to route around it
  by writing `scripts/build_joined_wide_table.py`. The one-line fix
  is in the finding; the broader fix-pass item is that the platform
  needs an "explicit ground-truth execution" knob, not a heuristic.
- Best-epoch checkpointing. With 41 % accuracy on cat-bird-dog
  being the bottleneck, the project would benefit from saving the
  best validation-acc model state and using *that* for the
  committed CSV rather than the final epoch. The validation lane is
  already wired through the training loop (per
  tk-005); only the save-best policy is missing.
- The analysis pictures are split across two executions
  ([ZW0](https://localhost/id/27/ZW0@355-RZWY-9B9R) for the
  notebook's n=250 ROC curves + confusion matrices, and
  [1012](https://localhost/id/27/1012@355-RZWY-9B9R) for the n=550
  joined table + per-class recall). A future fix-pass that lands
  the GT-heuristic fix would let one execution cover both.

The headline a non-ML reader should take away: **YHP is the most
accurate model, but use it carefully — when it's wrong it's
confidently wrong about 1 image in 3.** If the use case is triage
(where the analyst can review borderline cases manually), XCE's
better-calibrated confidence makes it the safer pick at the cost of
3 percentage points of accuracy.
